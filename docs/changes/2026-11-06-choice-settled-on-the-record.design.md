# Technical Design: The decider settles each choice on the record

Product part: [2026-11-06-choice-settled-on-the-record.requirements.md](2026-11-06-choice-settled-on-the-record.requirements.md).

## Summary

The decider's task gains one line per candidate: the choice its verifier leaves to the author, from the field `authorChoice` that `verdicts.recorded@3` already records. The decider's answer gains, on each decision, a list of settled choices, each `{for, choice, by, source}`, where `for` names the candidates of the finding whose verifiers' choice it answers and `by` is one of seven steps, the five of the rubric's order and two the rubric gives a fix outside it, and a fourth reason to leave a finding, `settled`. `checkDecisions` and the fold both hold the table of R2: which decision must answer each candidate that names a choice, may carry settled choices or must carry none, and with which steps. They also hold that no settled choice names a candidate that is not the finding's or that names no choice, and that no candidate is named twice. The ledger records the new answer as `decisions.recorded@2`, which any run may record, since a run records its decisions once, and the fold reads version 1 with no settled choice. Version 2 has not reached `main`, and the list changes its shape in place (TD6). The form drops the author rule, R9 of the review that reads as a report, for every run, a run decided at version 1 included. Wherever the form or the report shows a decision of `fix` or `leave`, it shows each choice that a candidate's verifier named beside the settled choice that names that candidate, or says that deep-review did not settle it, and a finding no longer has one choice for display. `checkDecisions` names in one refusal every rule that an answer breaks, which the decider's next attempt is given (R9), and the decider's task states the steps and the table of R2 in every run. The decider's rubric says that a fix edits and when a decision is left as `settled`, and that a choice between two behaviors that nothing stated settles is asked, with keeping the code as the default, even when the order would keep it. The rubric and the decider's task ask for each settled choice as one declarative sentence that states the side it settles, and the form labels it as what deep-review decided.

## Non-Goals

### NG1: No change to the verifier

`verifierOutputSchemaV3`, `verdicts.recorded@3`, `notesParagraph` in `src/review/tasks.ts` and `roles/fragments/rubrics.md` stay. The verifier's `Needs the author:` note stays in its evidence, so in a read-only run of a pull request the decider reads the choice twice, once as the field and once in the evidence.

### NG2: No change to the fixer's routes

`routeOfDecision` in `src/checkpoint/fix-state.ts:1019` already routes a `leave` to `held`, so a finding left as `settled` reaches no fixer, and routes to `held` too an `ask` whose applied option does not edit, so an ask whose default keeps the code reaches no fixer either. The fixer's task gains the settled choices of its decision and of each finding that its fix removes, with the verifiers' choices beside them, which "The fixer's task" below gives, and nothing else of the fixer changes.

### NG3: No change to what the writer explains

`findingsToExplain` in `src/review/explanation.ts` still gives the writer the findings decided `fix` or `ask`, and its answer and its checks stay. Its task gains the lines of the decision's settled choices, with the verifiers' choices beside them, which "The writer's task" below gives.

## Context

### The decider's task today

`deciderFindings` (`src/review/phases.ts:214`) gives each ranked finding its id, severity, verdict, summary, reason and candidates, each candidate through `taskCandidate` (`phases.ts:203`). `TaskCandidate` (`src/review/tasks.ts:578`) has no field for the verifier's two notes. `candidateLines` (`tasks.ts:732`) prints a candidate's summary, detail and evidence, and serves both the decider's task (`deciderTask`, `tasks.ts:1210`) and the fixer's (`tasks.ts:903`). The decider's role prompt is assembled from `decider-brief.md`, `worker-scope.md`, `lead-verify.md`, `rubrics.md` and `decider-rubric.md` (`roles/manifest.json:121`).

### The decision and its record

`deciderOutputSchema` (`src/review/schemas.ts:216`) gives each decision `{index, decision, grounds, fix, leave, ask, departure}`, with `edits` only on the options of a question. `checkDecisions` (`schemas.ts:597`) holds one part per decision kind, a departure on a fix only, the option indexes and the superseded rule, and throws a `StructuralCheckError` at the first rule that an answer breaks, which the phase records as the reason of an `attempt.failed` that begins `structural check:` (`phases.ts:797`). The decision phase records the checked answer as `decisions.recorded@1` (`phases.ts:893`), whose schema (`recordedDecisionSchema`, `src/checkpoint/events.ts:1350`) takes its leave reasons from the frozen `vocabularyV4.leaveReasons` (`events.ts:1227`). `decisionsRecorded` (`src/checkpoint/review-fold.ts:820`) holds them in the ranking's order. The engine's list of leave reasons is `leaveReasons` in `src/review/vocabulary.ts:544`, and a test of `test/roles/repository.test.ts` holds the rubric's list to it.

### The verifier's choice in the state

`resolutionOf` and `mergedResolution` (`src/review/state.ts:75`, `:137`) give a finding the `authorChoice` of its shown candidate, else of the first other candidate that names one. Verdicts recorded at version 1 or 2 fold with `authorChoice: null` (`review-fold.ts:780`). A run records its verdicts at version 3 only when it is configured at `review.configured@11`, a read-only run of a pull request (`verdictsVersionOf`, `review-fold.ts:728`).

### The author rule in the form

`forAuthor` (`src/review/pull-request-form.ts:250`) is true for a finding decided `leave` with an `authorChoice`. It puts the finding in full in the body (`inFull`, `:253`), gives it `authorBlocks` (`:453`) in `decisionBlocks` (`:468`), lists it in what needs a person (`:591`), counts it in the summary (`:565`), and keeps it out of `leftAsIs` (`:655`). Its words are `needsAuthor`, `authorChoice`, `whyLook` and the summary's count in `src/review/pull-request-words.ts`, in English and Korean. The report on the machine does not read `authorChoice`.

### The status of a fix

`findingStatusOf` (`src/review/brief.ts:369`) makes every `fix` of a read-only run `planned` or `planned-against-rule`, and the form counts and words it as a fix. In a fix run, `routeOfDecision` sends every `fix` to a fixer.

## Design

### The decider's task (R1, R2)

`DeciderTaskFinding` gives each candidate `authorChoice`, read from the candidate's verdict, null for a run whose verdicts lack it. `candidateLines` prints it when it is given, as `choice left to the author by <candidate id>'s verifier: <text>`, so a settled choice can name the candidate in `for`, after the evidence. The verdict's `unconfirmed` is not passed: no rule reads it, and the evidence line already ends with what keeps a `PLAUSIBLE` verdict from `CONFIRMED` (`roles/fragments/rubrics.md`). The fixer's task passes no `authorChoice` to its candidates, so its candidate lines do not change; the verifiers' choices reach the fixer beside the settlement, in its decided lines ("The fixer's task" below). `deciderTask`'s text names the line and the settled choices of the answer, each of which names in `for` the ids of the candidates whose choice it answers, as the task prints those ids. It says that a settled choice's `choice` states, in one declarative sentence, the side that the decision settles, not the question, and gives one example of such a sentence, such as "Ids in the writer's text are replaced by the numbers the body shows." It says too that the choice and the source are in plain words for a person who did not read the change (PD10 of the requirements). It also states the seven steps, one line each, the rules of `for`, and the table of R2 of the requirements, which says which decision must answer each candidate that names a choice, may carry settled choices or must carry none, and with which steps. The task states them because `deciderTask` is engine code that every run receives, while a run resumed with its old roles directory has a rubric that defines neither the steps nor `settled` (RK4 of the requirements).

### A refused answer, named to the next attempt (R9)

`checkDecisions` collects every rule that an answer breaks, the rules of today and those of R2 and R3, each worded with the index of the decision and the rule, and throws one `StructuralCheckError` that lists them within `maxRecordedTextLength`, the limit of a recorded reason, in place of throwing at the first. The phase records it as today. The decision phase builds the next attempt's task from the failures that the decision unit holds (`UnitFailure` in `src/checkpoint/review-fold.ts:121`, which the fold keeps from each `attempt.failed`), keeping those whose `cause` is `unit` and whose reason is a structural-check failure, and `deciderTask` names their reasons under a section "What failed in the earlier answer" and asks for the whole answer again, as `writerTask` does for the parts it drops (`src/review/explanation.ts:998`). The failures are cleared when a blocked phase is resumed, so a resumed run starts its decider without them, as the fold does for every unit today; the requirements accept this (R9). No event changes.

### The answer and its check (R2, R3)

`deciderOutputSchema` adds to each decision `settled`, a list of `{for: candidate ids, choice: text up to 400, by: settleStep, source: text up to 400}`, empty when the decision settles no choice, where `settleSteps` in `vocabulary.ts` is `['intent', 'contract', 'before', 'kept', 'shape', 'scope', 'none']`: the five steps of the rubric's order in its order, then the scope of a fix that keeps to the part inside the change, and no choice remaining. `leaveReasons` becomes `['outside-change-not-regression', 'superseded', 'intended', 'settled']`. `checkDecisions(output, findings)` takes, for each index, the ids of the finding's candidates, its primary and its members, and which of them have a verdict that names an `authorChoice`, and holds the table of R2 of the requirements. It refuses an answer in which:

- a decision of `fix` with no departure, or of `leave` as `intended`, `settled` or `superseded`, leaves a candidate whose verdict names a choice out of the `for` of every settled choice;
- a settled choice names in `for` a candidate that is not the finding's, or one whose verdict names no choice;
- a candidate is named twice in the `for` lists of one decision;
- a decision of `leave` as `settled` has no settled choice, whatever its finding's candidates name;
- a decision of `leave` as `outside-change-not-regression`, or of `ask`, has one;
- a settled choice names a step that its decision does not allow: `intended` allows `intent`, `contract` and `shape`, `settled` allows `shape`, `superseded` allows every step but `before`, and `fix`, with or without a departure, allows all seven;
- a decision of `leave` as `intended` carries settled choices and names `intent` or `contract` in none of them.

A `fix` with a departure may name some of the candidates that name a choice, all of them or none, under the same rules of `for`, and a settled choice with an empty `for` settles a choice that no verifier named.

The rule of each case in the table holds, beside the steps it allows, the steps that at least one of its settled choices must name when the decision carries any; only `intended` sets them, to `intent` and `contract` (OQ3). The condition is read from the table with the rest, by `checkDecisions` and the fold alike, and the decider's task states it in the line of its case.

### The event and the fold (TD1, TD3, TD5, TD6)

`decisionsRecordedV2` holds `recordedDecisionSchemaV2`: each variant of version 1 with `settled`, the list of settled choices of the answer, beside `departure`, and the leave reasons of a new frozen vocabulary, `reviewVocabularyV13.leaveReasons`, with `settled`. Each settled choice's `by` takes `reviewVocabularyV13.settleSteps`, and its `for` is a list of candidate ids. The fold applies to version 2 the same rules as to version 1, and every rule of R2 and R3 that `checkDecisions` holds, those that depend on the verifiers' choices included. A finding's candidates are its primary and its members as the ranking records them, and a candidate names a choice when its verdict records an `authorChoice` at version 3; the fold checks each id of a `for` against them (TD3). The reducer of version 1 gives every decision an empty list of settled choices. The review state does not record the version that its decisions were recorded at, since nothing reads it (TD5). This shape of version 2 replaces, in place, its first shape, which held at most one settled choice for each decision and no `for` (TD6).

### The rubric (R1, R2, R3)

`decider-rubric.md` changes in seven places, in a commit of its own:

1. Its first paragraph names the choice left to the author as a line of the candidate, and still calls it a verifier's view to settle or ask about, not an answer.
2. "Settling a choice" lists the five steps of the order as list items in the form ``- `intent`: the change's stated intent``, in place of its numbered list, as a list that a test of `repository.test.ts` reads. The paragraph on a choice between two shapes that only a maintainer would see becomes the fifth item, `shape`, with its whole rule: the one the surrounding code already uses, else the one that adds less. R2's words for `shape` are this rule. Two more list items follow, marked as the steps outside the order, `scope` and then `none`, so the section lists the seven steps in the order of `settleSteps`.
3. The table of the three decisions says that a `fix` edits the code, names in its `fix` row the two steps outside the order, `none` for "acting needs no choice" and `scope` for the part of a result inside the change, and says that a choice the order settles without an edit is not fixed: it is left as `intended` or `settled`, or, when it is a choice between two behaviors that a caller or a user would see and only keeping a behavior that was there before settles it, asked, with a default that keeps the code and does not edit. Its `ask` row names that case, and its `leave` row says four reasons, not three.
4. Its list of reasons to leave gains `settled`, in the form the test reads: "`settled`: acting needs a choice between two shapes that only a maintainer reading the code would see, nothing stated settles it, and the rule for shapes keeps the code as it is; give the settled choice, by `shape`." The text of `intended` gains its second case: a choice that the change's stated intent or the repository's stated contract settles in favor of the code as it is, given with its settled choice by `intent` or `contract`, and beside it, on a finding whose candidates also name a choice between two shapes that the rule for shapes keeps, that choice by `shape` (OQ3). The text of `superseded` says to give, when its verifiers named choices, the settled choices that the superseding fix makes for the finding, by any step but `before`, and the text of `outside-change-not-regression` says that such a finding carries no settled choice.
5. A sentence after "Your decision names the choice and its answer" says to record each choice that the decision settles as one settled choice in `settled`, with the ids of the candidates whose choice it answers in `for`, its step and the source it rests on, and that where a settlement is required each candidate with a choice left to the author is named by exactly one settled choice; that a decision that settles no choice gives `settled` empty; that the choice states, in one declarative sentence, the side that the decision settles, not the question, with one example; that the choice and the source are written for a person who did not read the change, in plain words with no file, line, requirement number or internal name of the code, while the grounds cite, without the words "as you write a question" that the rubric first used; and that a `fix` with a departure may record its choice in the departure instead.
6. The paragraph on a result that lies partly past the change names `scope` as the step of such a fix.
7. The section of the three decisions ends with the table of R2 of the requirements, which decision must answer each candidate that names a choice, may carry settled choices or must carry none, and with which steps, with the reason of each row.

The rubric then has two lists in the same form, the steps and the reasons to leave, and the test that holds the reasons to `leaveReasons` reads the whole fragment today (`test/roles/repository.test.ts:332`), so it would take the steps for reasons. Each of the two tests therefore reads only its own section, which it slices with `indexOf` from the section's first line to the next heading, as the test of the severities slices its part of `phase4-list.md`. The test of the reasons reads only the section that begins "The reasons a finding is left, and no others:" and holds its labels to `leaveReasons`. A new test reads only the section "Settling a choice" and holds its labels, in order, to `settleSteps`.

### The form (R4, R5, R7)

`forAuthor` and `authorBlocks` are removed for every run, with the item for the author in what needs a person, the summary's count `authors`, and their words `needsAuthor`, `whyLook`, the word `authorChoice` and the summary's sentences on findings left for the author, in English and Korean. A finding decided `leave` with a candidate that names a choice then goes in `leftAsIs` in every run, is not in full in the body unless it was there for another reason, and is not counted or listed as for the author.

`mergedResolution` (`src/review/state.ts:137`) no longer picks one `authorChoice` for a finding. The form's view of a finding carries instead the choice of each of its candidates that names one, with the candidate's id, the primary first and then the members in the ranking's order. The choice paragraph of a finding (`choiceLines`) is built from that list and the decision's settled choices. For each settled choice, it gives the choices of the candidates in its `for`, each labeled as a verifier's and each distinct text once, then the settled choice with `words.settleSteps[by]` and its source; a settled choice whose `for` is empty is given alone. The settled choice's label, in both languages, states it as what deep-review decided, such as "deep-review decided:" in English, in place of "deep-review settles it:", which read as if the open question followed (PD10 of the requirements). Then the paragraph gives each candidate's choice that no settled choice names, labeled as a verifier's, with the statement that deep-review did not settle it, again each distinct text once. A finding whose candidates name no choice and whose decision settles none has no such paragraph. Each line of the findings left as is gives the paragraph after the reason and the grounds.

The `left` cut of `bodyCuts` (`pull-request-form.ts:111`) changes with it: the cut keeps the lines of the findings with a candidate that names a choice, the rule of R4, and words the others as "the other N findings left as is", a new entry of the words in English and Korean, so a body over the limit still shows each choice that a verifier named. `words.leftCut` stays for a cut that keeps no line, when no finding left as is has a candidate that names a choice. The lines that a kept finding adds grow with its choices: up to 400 characters of the workers' text for each distinct verifier's choice (`verifierNoteLengthV1`), and up to 800 for each settled choice and its source; the cuts after `left` still apply if the body stays too long. `decisionBlocks` gives a `fix` the same paragraph by the same rule after the sentence of what deep-review would do, or for a minor finding after its paragraph, in its comment and in its text in the body. The folded table of minor findings does not change, since its cell of what deep-review would do is one sentence. An ask's question is the record of its choice, so `decisionBlocks` gives an `ask` no such paragraph. The words gain `settleSteps` in English and Korean, the words of the choice paragraph for a list of settled choices and for the choices that none names, the words of a cut that keeps some lines, and the label of `settled` in `leaveReasons`.

### The writer's task (R5)

The writer's task prints each candidate's verifier choice through `noteLines` (`explanation.ts:876`), and the decision through `decisionLines` (`explanation.ts:851`), which shows no settlement. A writer that explains a fix could then present the verifier's choice as open beside the engine's line that says how it is settled. `decisionLines` gains, for each settled choice of the decision, the choices of the candidates in its `for`, each labeled as a verifier's and each distinct text once, then a line `settled by the decision:` with the choice, the step and the source, labeled as the decision's, where the step is given in the English words that the report on the machine uses for it, not as its raw value. Then it gives each candidate's choice that no settled choice names, labeled as a verifier's and as not settled by deep-review. The task says that a choice the decision settled is not the author's to make. No check of the answer changes.

### The fixer's task (R5)

The fixer's task prints each candidate's evidence (`candidateLines`, `tasks.ts:738`), which in a read-only run of a pull request still ends with the verifier's `Needs the author:` note, and such a run can be continued into a fix pass with `--fix`. `decidedLines` (`tasks.ts:750`) prints the grounds, the approach, the rejected options and the departure, and no settlement. A fixer could then hold the verifier's open choice beside an approach that does not spell out how the decision settled it, and make the choice itself, which principle 2 does not permit. `decidedLines` gains, for a `fix`, the same lines as the writer's task: for each settled choice, the verifiers' choices that it answers and a line `settled:` with the choice, the step in words and the source; then each verifier's choice that no settled choice names, marked as not settled by deep-review. The fixer then applies each choice the decision made, and sees which verifier's choice the decision did not settle. Each `removes also:` line (`tasks.ts:906`), which names a finding left as `superseded` that the fix removes, gains that finding's settled choices, each with the verifiers' choices it answers, in the same form, since the fix acts on that finding too. The verifiers' choices reach the fixer only in these lines, beside the settlement, and not as a field of a candidate, so the fixer's candidate lines do not change. A fix with a departure need not settle its verifiers' choices, so its fixer can be given a choice marked as not settled and can still read the verifier's note in the evidence; the requirements accept this gap under principle 2.

### The report on the machine (R6)

`decision-report.ts` gains the label of `settled` and gives, after the grounds of each decision of `fix` or `leave`, the same lines as the form by the same rule, in English, since the report is in English today. It reads the choice of each candidate of the finding, with the candidate's id, from the review state, as the form does, and not one `authorChoice` of the merged resolution.

### An older run (R7)

A run decided at version 1 folds with an empty list of settled choices on every decision and is rendered as any other. Each choice that a candidate of a finding names is then shown with the statement that deep-review did not settle it, on the line of a finding left as is in `leftAsIs` and in the comment of a fix. A run with verdicts at version 1 or 2 has no `authorChoice`, so its findings carry none of these lines, at either version of its decisions.

### The golden fixture

The change adds `decisions.recorded@2` and a vocabulary version, so a fixture `schema-1-20` is committed with `npm run golden -- --output test/fixtures/checkpoints/schema-1-20`, and the older fixtures still open and fold. The runs of `scripts/golden-checkpoint.ts` all record their decisions at version 1 today, so a fixture made from them would hold no version 2 and prove nothing about a later engine reading one. The script gains a run whose decisions are at version 2, with a `settled` leave, an `intended` leave with a settled choice, a fix with a settled choice and a leave as outside the change of a finding whose verifier named a choice, and `golden.test.ts` holds what that run folds to, as it holds each earlier run that brought a new version. The run's decisions give `settled` as a list, and one of its findings merges two candidates whose verifiers named different choices, each named by its own settled choice, so the fixture holds a member's choice answered by name. The fixture `schema-1-20` was first committed on this branch with version 2 in its first shape, one settled choice for each decision; it is regenerated in place with the same command, not followed by `schema-1-21` (TD6).

Two tests hold today's leave reasons through the frozen vocabulary: `test/checkpoint/events-vocabulary.test.ts:382` and `:507` hold `reviewVocabularyV4` equal to `leaveReasons`, and `test/review/vocabulary.test.ts:60` pins the list. The first two hold `reviewVocabularyV4` to a literal of the three reasons instead, as the file holds an older list of phases to `phasesBeforeAnswers`, and a new assertion holds `reviewVocabularyV13` to today's `leaveReasons` and `settleSteps`. ## Technical Decisions

### TD1: A new version of `decisions.recorded`, which any run may record

`verdicts.recorded` follows the run's configuration, because a run records many of them and must never mix two answers (R9 of runs on the record). `explanation.recorded` follows it too, though a run records it once: a run of a pull request that an earlier engine configured keeps its writer's answer as it was when this engine resumes it (`events.ts:2722`, `review-fold.ts:855`). Decisions depart from that precedent. Tying them to the configuration would take a new version of `review.configured` for each of the two configurations an engine writes today, version 11 for a read-only run of a pull request, which explains its review, and version 9 for every other run (`controller.ts:1414`), to spare a run configured before the change and resumed after it the new answer. A run records its decisions once, so it cannot mix two versions, and the fold accepts either version in any run without a new version of `review.configured`. The engine of this change always records version 2. A run configured before the change and resumed after it with its old roles records version 2 too, from a decider whose rubric does not name the settled choice, its steps or `settled`. The schema still asks for the field, the decider's task states the steps and the table of R2 ("The decider's task" above), and a check that refuses the answer runs the decider again with what failed (R9 and RK3 of the requirements).

### TD2: The step is an enum that the engine words, and the source is text

The seven steps are the rubric's order and the two other ways its table reaches `fix`, so an enum costs no judgment the decider does not already make, and it lets the form give the step in the review's language and the gate count the steps across runs (PD6 of the requirements). The source is the decider's sentence in plain words, which the review shows, and the grounds keep its citation. The choice is the decider's sentence too, and states the side that the decision settles; the engine checks the form of neither sentence, since text is not a contract (PD10 of the requirements).

### TD3: The phase and the fold both hold the rules of the settlement

`checkDecisions` refuses an answer that breaks R2 or R3, so the decider is run again before anything is recorded, and the fold refuses a `decisions.recorded@2` that breaks them, so no engine fault and no hand-edited ledger can record one. This is how `verdicts.recorded@3` holds that a `PLAUSIBLE` verdict says what keeps it from `CONFIRMED`: the verifier's check and the fold both hold it (`recordVerdicts` in `review-fold.ts`). Which candidates of a finding name a choice is a fact of the ledger: the primary and the members that the ranking records, and their verdicts at version 3. The fold checks each id that a settled choice names against that fact, and finds each candidate that names a choice and that no settled choice names, so it holds the rules of `for` from the ledger alone and reads nothing that the form chooses to show.

The cost is that the rules of version 2 are fixed once version 2 is on `main`: an older ledger must always fold, so a later change to what a settlement must hold, such as a new step, is a new version of the event, not an edit of these rules. Two changes are made to them before that, both in place as TD6 gives: the list of settled choices, and the steps of a leave as `intended`, which the answer to OQ3 widens.

### TD4: A frozen vocabulary for the new reason

`decisions.recorded@1` reads its leave reasons from `reviewVocabularyV4`, so adding `settled` to `leaveReasons` in `vocabulary.ts` does not change the old schema. Version 2 reads them, and the seven steps, from `reviewVocabularyV13`, the next frozen vocabulary after `reviewVocabularyV12`, which a test holds equal to today's vocabulary as it holds each earlier one. A newer engine still reads an older ledger, and the golden test sees the change.

### TD5: The form does not read the version of the decisions

The author rule is removed for every run (PD7 of the requirements), so nothing in the form depends on the version at which a run recorded its decisions. A run decided at version 1 folds with no settled choice on any decision, and the form shows each choice that its verifiers named as not settled by deep-review, which is true of it. The review state needs no `decisionsVersion`, and the fold keeps no field for it.

### TD6: Version 2 of the decisions changes in place before its first merge

AGENTS.md asks that a changed payload shape be a new version of its event, never an edit of the old schema, because a newer engine must always read an older ledger; and that a change to the ledger be proven by a new golden fixture at the next serial, while the older fixtures stay. The list of settled choices (R2 and PD9 of the requirements) changes the payload of `decisions.recorded@2`. This design changes version 2 in place rather than adding a version 3, with the rules that the fold holds for it, and regenerates the fixture `schema-1-20` rather than following it with `schema-1-21`. It departs from both rules.

Both rules protect a ledger that an engine has written and a later engine must read. Version 2 was added by this pull request and has never been on `main`, and the only ledgers recorded at it are the checkpoints of the four runs of the first gate, which the author keeps outside the repository and which a second gate replaces. A version 3 would keep in the engine for good a version 2 that only those four ledgers recorded, with its schema, its reducer and its rules, and each later reader of the fold would have to learn why it is there. The fixture `schema-1-20` was committed on this branch only and holds version 2 in its first shape, which the engine of this change refuses, so it could not stay as a fixture that must still open and fold; and a fixture of a shape that no merged engine wrote proves nothing about a newer engine reading an older ledger.

The design accepts that the engine of this change refuses the decisions of the first gate's four ledgers, so it cannot open those runs. Each of them reached its report, and the numbers that the gate relies on are recorded under Verification. The answer to OQ3 changes the rules that the fold holds for version 2 in the same way, before its first merge: a leave as `intended` may name `shape` beside a step that is a statement. The rules change in place for the reasons above, and they only widen what a leave as `intended` may hold, since every set of settled choices that the earlier rules accepted names only `intent` and `contract`, or none, so a ledger that they folded still folds.

Once this change is on `main`, version 2 is fixed as every other version is, and a later change to its shape is a version 3 (TD3).

## Open Questions

### OQ1: Closed: does the decider fill the settled choice when its verifier named none?

Closed, since the design answers it. Where the verifier named no choice, R2's table makes a settled choice optional on a fix, an `intended` leave and a `superseded` leave, requires one on a `settled` leave, and the rubric invites the decider to record each choice it settles; such a settled choice names no candidate in `for`. The form, the report and the tasks of the writer and the fixer show a settled choice wherever a decision carries one, whether or not its verifier named a choice ("The form", "The writer's task", "The fixer's task" and "The report on the machine" above), so nothing is left to decide about showing it.

### OQ2: Should the verifier stop writing `Needs the author:` in the evidence of a read-only run?

The decider now reads the choice as a field, so the note in the evidence is read twice in those runs. Removing it changes the verifier's rubric, which NG1 of the requirements leaves out. It is a candidate for the change that tightens how readily a verifier names a choice.

### OQ3: Closed: how is a finding left as is when its verifiers' choices need the steps of two reasons?

Closed by the author's decision. A finding has one decision, and each settled choice of a leave names a step that the leave's reason allows (R2 of the requirements). A finding can merge a candidate whose choice the repository's stated contract settles in favor of the code and a candidate whose choice is between two shapes that the rule for shapes keeps. With `intended` allowing only `intent` and `contract`, and `settled` only `shape`, such a finding fit neither reason, so unless a fix removed it as `superseded`, its decider could only ask, though the repository answers both choices, or have its answer refused.

A leave as `intended` now allows `intent`, `contract` and `shape`, on the condition that at least one of its settled choices is by `intent` or `contract`; a leave as `settled` still allows `shape` only (R2, R3 and PD4 of the requirements). A finding that the repository's statement settles in part is left as the repository states it, which is what `intended` says, and the condition keeps `intended` resting on a statement, so a leave whose every choice is a shape is still `settled`. `settled` keeps its meaning of a shape settled with nothing stated, and M3 still counts the two apart. The case was resolved before a run showed how often it occurs, since a refused answer stops the run (RK3 of the requirements) and the widening costs one field of the table and refuses nothing that the earlier rules accepted. Version 2 of the decisions has never been on `main`, so its rules change in place, with no new version of the event (TD6).

## Test Strategy

### TS1: The decider's task (R1, R2)

A test of `deciderTask` that gives a candidate with an `authorChoice` and an `unconfirmed` and holds the line of the choice and no line of `unconfirmed`, and one that gives the fixer's task the same candidate and holds that the line of the choice is not printed. A test holds that the decider's task states each of the seven steps on a line of its own, each row of the table of R2, and the rules of `for`, and that it asks for the choice as one declarative sentence of the side that the decision settles, with its example.

### TS2: The answer and its check (R2, R3)

One table-driven test runs every case of the table of R2 through both `checkDecisions` and the fold. The cases are each kind of decision, an ask, a fix with and without a departure, and a leave for each of its four reasons, on a finding whose verifier named a choice and on one whose verifier named none, with no settled choice and with a settled choice by each of the seven steps. The test holds that the phase and the fold each accept or refuse every case as the table says. It also runs the rules of `for`, under each decision, on a finding merged from two candidates whose verifiers named choices: both named by one settled choice, each by its own, one named by none, one named twice, a settled choice that names a candidate of another finding, one that names a candidate whose verifier named no choice, and one whose `for` is empty. On the same merged finding it runs a leave as `intended` that settles one candidate's choice by `contract` and the other's by `shape`, which both accept; one that settles both by `shape`, which both refuse; and a leave as `settled` that settles one by `contract`, which both refuse (OQ3). A test of the decision phase holds that it records version 2 and passes to `checkDecisions` each finding's candidates and which of them name a choice, in place of the test that pins version 1 (`test/review/phases.test.ts:428`). The test of `repository.test.ts` that holds the rubric's leave reasons to `leaveReasons` reads only the section that begins "The reasons a finding is left, and no others:" and covers the fourth reason, and a new one reads only the section "Settling a choice" and holds its labels, in order, to `settleSteps`.

### TS3: The event and the fold (R2, TD1, TD3)

Tests of the fold that read `decisions.recorded` at versions 1 and 2 in one kind of run each, and give no settled choice for version 1. ### TS4: The form (R4, R5, R7)

The four tests of `test/review/pull-request-form.test.ts` that pin the author rule are rewritten to the new behavior: on a run decided at version 1, a finding left as is whose verifier named a choice is not in what needs a person, not counted for the author, and in the folded section with the verifier's choice and the statement that deep-review did not settle it. A test of a fix with a departure, a verifier's choice and no settled choice holds the verifier's choice and that statement in its comment, and a test of an ask whose verifier named a choice holds that its block shows neither. A test posts a finding merged from two candidates whose verifiers named different choices, each named by its own settled choice, and holds each verifier's choice beside its own settled line; a test in which two candidates named the same words holds the text once; and a test of a fix with a departure that names one of two candidates holds the other's choice with the statement that deep-review did not settle it. A test of a body cut at `left` holds that the lines of the findings with a candidate whose verifier named a choice stay, including a finding whose choice only a member's verifier named, and that the others are worded as the other findings left as is, and a test in which no finding left as is has a verifier's choice holds `words.leftCut`. ### TS5: The writer's and the fixer's tasks (R5)

A test of the writer's task that gives a fix of a merged finding with two settled choices and holds, under the decision, each verifier's choice beside the settled line that names its candidate, with the step in words and not its raw value. One of the fixer's task holds the same lines in its decided lines, a verifier's choice marked as not settled on a fix with a departure that names no candidate, and the settled choices of a finding left as `superseded` on its `removes also:` line.

### TS6: The report (R6)

A test of `decision-report.ts` that gives a `settled` leave whose verifier named a choice and holds its label, the verifier's choice and its settled line, one that gives a leave as outside the change whose verifier named a choice and holds the statement that deep-review did not settle it, and one that gives a merged finding whose two candidates are named by two settled choices and holds each verifier's choice beside its own.

### TS7: The gate (R8)

The gate's runs, recorded under Verification with the metrics of the requirements, whether each met its target, and whether the run of #96's change reached the finding of #107 or left that case untested.

### TS8: A refused answer, named to the next attempt (R9)

A test of `checkDecisions` that gives an answer breaking two rules in two decisions and holds that one refusal names both, each with its decision's index. A test of the decision phase whose first answer is refused, and that holds the second task to name, under "What failed in the earlier answer", the refused rules with their indexes, and not a failure of another cause.
